Last week, I volunteered as an interpreter at UNFCCC Climate Week (UN Climate Change).

For four days, I met people I would not normally have had the chance to meet: CEOs, people working in government and international organizations, professors, delegates, organizers, volunteers, and many others moving toward their own goals.

The clearest lesson I took from the week was simple:

If there is something you want to do, knock on the door and take the next step.

One conversation with Alexander Demissie made that lesson concrete.

When I told him I wanted to hear more about how the UN is thinking about AI in the climate context, he did not overcomplicate it. His advice was direct: go, talk to people, and get connected.

Because of that conversation, my volunteer position was adjusted, and I was able to attend the session I had wanted to hear.

That stayed with me.

During the week, I spoke with people working across climate, AI, policy, education, and entrepreneurship. Some were already far along in their careers. Some were still exploring. But many of them had one thing in common: they had chosen to move before everything was perfectly clear.

That was useful for me to see.

I often think too much about whether I am ready, qualified, or prepared enough. This experience reminded me that opportunities do not always come after certainty. Sometimes they come after you show up, ask, listen, and keep moving.

I am grateful to everyone I met during the week, and to the UNFCCC Climate Week team for the opportunity to contribute as a volunteer interpreter.

It was a great exprience woring with Zhanna Tlegenova , Da-in Song, Sihyun Park, Hun Jin
