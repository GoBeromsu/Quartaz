Last week I joined the Net Zero Data Challenge 2025, organised by the Yorkshire & Humber Office for Data Analytics (YHODA) and South Yorkshire Sustainability Centre. It was my first hackathon experience in the UK — and a truly eye-opening one. Even more exciting, our solution was selected as the most impactful of the challenge

As the only developer in our team, I took responsibility for the full technical pipeline: from integrating multiple air quality and traffic datasets, to applying machine learning methods I had studied during The University of Sheffield master’s, and finally building an interactive dashboard that decision-makers could explore directly.

What made this challenge special was the collaboration :)  
Robyn Farr, Isaac Coomer, and Talia Blain provided crucial policy insights around Clean Air Zone schemes. Josh Zacharias led the team with strong momentum and direction.

My role was to translate these ideas into a working product, ensuring our solution was grounded in real data and usable by stakeholders.

The hardest part wasn’t just the data cleaning or modelling, but bringing together so many good ideas under time pressure. I realised that communication and coordination can be more difficult — and more important — than the technical work itself.

For me, this hackathon was not only about producing results. It was about seeing how academic knowledge can transform into practical solutions, and about learning how diverse expertise — technical, policy, strategic — can come together to address complex local challenges.

I’m grateful to my team and to the organisers (YHODA & SYSC) for giving us this opportunity. This experience strengthened my belief that data science, when applied collaboratively, can genuinely contribute to solving social and environmental problems.
